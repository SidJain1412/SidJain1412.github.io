Almost every developer you know can now make personal software with the help of AI.

- **Splitwise is asking for a Pro plan?** Make your own version!
- **Personal website stuck in 2016?** Weekend project: update it!
- **Enjoy playing FPL?** [Train a model to optimise your team!](https://fplguru.com/)
- **Don't like Cursor?** [Make your own IDE!](https://github.com/judepereira/jupiter)

Devs around me have done these things. These were personal itches, and one day they just realised that AI could really help solve them. So they rolled up their sleeves and got to it!

I am sure people around you are doing the same.

But are non-developers catching up at the pace we thought they would?

My not-so-technical friends have problems that AI can easily help with too:

- Figure out what to cook, accounting for dozens of different allergies and dietary preferences.
- Build a personal marketing dashboard that finds content opportunities and generates campaign ideas every week.
- Turn messy client notes into a polished weekly report.

Somehow, they are hesitant about getting started, despite me rambling to them about throwing the problem statement at an LLM and just going for it.

The average person around me still uses ChatGPT to plan a vacation or create AI images. They simply aren't even thinking of asking an LLM to create software for them. It's a nearly completely unknown domain to them, so it's understandable.

IMO, making simple software is no longer the biggest bottleneck. **Awareness, determination and hosting are.**

## Awareness

By awareness, I don't mean knowing that LLMs can make software. I mean knowing what they can really help you achieve if you give them a good problem statement and spend a few hours with them.

Someone who's never built software will naturally think that making a recipe app is a tough task, because it's something they've only ever seen on the App Store with 5 million downloads. They'll think, "Of course I won't be able to make something like that."

> (Good read: [How to Be More Ambitious: A Tutorial for Humans With Models](https://felixrieseberg.com/how-to-be-more-ambitious-a-tutorial-for-humans-with-models/))

## Determination

Devs know what can be achieved when you take a seemingly hard problem and just keep at it for a while.

Understandably, someone who has never opened a terminal won't be comfortable suddenly opening an IDE and watching AI write thousands of lines of code and run weird commands. To them, it looks like a hacking scene from a bad 80s movie.

But getting past that first hump is important.

Once someone kicks off their first project, they understand just how much they can get done. I have even seen them being more determined than devs, because we come with biases about what can work and what can't, while the non-technical person feels starry-eyed after achieving something they didn't think they could!

**So, determination can be a consequence, not a prerequisite.**

On the other hand, some people just won't find creating software fulfilling. That's understandable too.

## Hosting

Even devs struggle with this part.

Great personal software is nice, but if it only lives in ChatGPT or Claude, it may not be so meaningful. Truly simple hosting (by simple, I mean non-daunting, cheap and reliable) is being tackled by companies like Vercel, Railway and Emergent.

But it's still scary for someone to whom uploading code to GitHub or even using a terminal is alien territory.

The gap between "I made an app with Claude" and "Here's a link to an app I made!" is vast (even for many developers).

Cost can also be an issue (especially in India), because even if it's cheap, some itches just don't justify spending $5–10 a month on them.

## So when does the revolution occur?

Startups like Emergent are showing traction. AI adoption is going up. Tech-adjacent people are getting more hands-on with code.

But the average person still has a mental leap to make.

**Software has always been something you downloaded or bought, not made.**

Developers have started unlearning that. I wonder how long it'll take everyone else.
